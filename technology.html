RGB, CMYK, and ECG: three different ways to print color

Most ECG presses lay OGV (orange, green, violet) on top of CMYK as a gamut extension. Sinso runs a native RGB workflow on a Heidelberg Speedmaster XL 75 — there is no CMYK base layer underneath the extended inks. The result is roughly 85% of the visible color gamut, versus ~52% for standard CMYK and ~72% for typical hybrid ECG.

Why no CMYK base layer matters

In a standard ECG workflow, the press still prints CMYK as the foundation and uses OGV inks to push beyond what CMYK can reach. That works — but the CMYK base limits how saturated the brightest colors can become, especially deep violets, vivid oranges, and saturated greens.

Sinso's workflow generates separations directly from the RGB artwork, then prints the seven inks needed to render that gamut on a single pass through the XL 75. The deep blues and saturated oranges that fall outside CMYK are printed at full strength, not approximated.

Where the limits are

Native RGB printing is not the right answer for every job. Brand-neutral folding cartons that ship in volume — clamshells, plain mailers, basic retail boxes — are usually better produced on a conventional CMYK line. RGB-native printing is worth the workflow when color fidelity is part of the product: artist editions, designer toy packaging, beauty SKUs where the unboxing color is part of the brand promise.
